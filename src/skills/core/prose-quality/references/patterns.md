# Prose patterns to cut

Each entry names the pattern, shows it, and gives the fix. Detect mode quotes
these names.

## Words

**Inflated vocabulary.** delve, foster, utilize, facilitate, empower,
streamline, cutting-edge, game changer, paradigm shift, tapestry, beacon,
multifaceted, meticulous, intricate, paramount, transformative, elevate, embark,
supercharge, seamless, unlock, ever-evolving, testament. Use the plain word
(use, help, start, careful) or cut. Technical senses stay (see SKILL.md).

**Empty adverbs.** just, simply, actually, truly, literally, fundamentally,
crucially, importantly, inherently, seamlessly. Cut unless they carry contrast,
real uncertainty or the writer's spoken rhythm.

**Filler phrases.** it's worth noting, it's important to note, at its core, when
it comes to, in today's world, in the age of, the reality is, in terms of, in
order to, going forward, let's dive in, at the end of the day. Delete and start
at the point.

**Weak verb phrases.** "made a decision" -> decided, "has the ability to" ->
can, "serves as" / "stands as" -> is.

## Sentences

**Binary contrast.** "It's not X, it's Y." / "The question isn't X but Y." State
Y.

**Negative listing.** "Not a X. Not a Y. A Z." Say Z.

**Throat-clearing opener.** "Here's the thing", "Let me be clear", "Here's what
nobody tells you", "The part everyone misses". Cut; make the claim stand alone.

**Colon reveal.** "The best part: it learns." Write the plain sentence. Colons
are for lists, labels and quotes.

**Rhetorical setup.** "What if I told you", "Think about it", "Plot twist", a
question the next sentence answers. Make the point.

**Superficial -ing tail.** "..., highlighting / underscoring / showcasing /
reflecting X." Replace with the actual consequence or cut.

**Importance puffery.** "marks a pivotal moment", "plays a vital role",
"underscores its significance". State the fact; the reader judges weight.

**Interpretive metadiscourse.** "This matters more than it sounds", "The key
point is", "As you can see", "In other words" repeating what was clear. Delete.

**Weasel attribution.** "Experts agree", "studies show", "many argue". Name the
source or flag the claim.

**Inanimate agent.** "The decision emerged", "the data tells us". Name who did
it.

**Synonym cycling.** agent / assistant / tool for the same thing. Repeat the
right word.

**Dramatic fragments.** "X. And Y. And Z." / "That's it. That's the whole
thing." Use full sentences.

**Rule of three.** Reflexive triads of adjectives or clauses. Two items, or the
one that matters.

**Robotic rhythm.** Consecutive sentences of the same length and shape, every
paragraph built the same way. Vary where it helps the point.

**Fake-profound kicker.** A closing aphorism or metaphor. Delete it; end on the
last concrete sentence. Do not rewrite it into a better metaphor.

**Recap ending.** "In conclusion", "Overall", "Ultimately", a paragraph that
restates the piece. End on the last point or the next action.

**Sycophantic frame.** "Great question", "I hope this helps", "Happy to help",
"Let me know if you have any questions". Delete.

## Formatting

**Decorative formatting.** Emoji in headings, bold scattered mid-sentence,
bullets where two sentences read better, headers over two-sentence sections,
tables for three facts. Format follows content.

**Em dash habit.** A dash as the default rhythm device. None in short copy; in
long text keep one or two only where they beat a comma, period or parentheses.
